# 011 — Episodes: the long form

The analysis behind WORLD-0006 and MEM-0002. The survey that motivated them is
`docs/research/03-recursive-micro-simulation.md`; the measurement is
`ops/episodes.md`, regenerated by `make episodes`.

## 1. What was actually missing

WORLD-0003's encounter is a good mechanism and a complete dead end for this
question. One `agent.tick` request per person per tick asks where they go,
whether they stop to talk, to whom, about what, their mood, and — when it is
possible — whether to press the vendor. Then it resolves, once.

Two consequences follow, and both are structural rather than a matter of tuning:

1. **A conversation cannot develop.** Jev answers the questions in a request
   independently, so "whom did I just talk to" cannot inform "what do I do now"
   inside one call. With one call per tick per person, nothing carries.
2. **There is nowhere to put a result.** `raise_outage` reaches `ticket.escalated`
   and that is the entire vocabulary of consequence. `topic` is chosen and, apart
   from citing the incident, read by nothing; `mood` lands in `positions`.

So the honest statement of the gap was not "encounters are too coarse". It was:
*the world has one channel from a meeting to the economy, and a richer meeting
would have nothing to push down it.* That is why MEM-0002 is a prerequisite and
not a sibling — it is the somewhere.

## 2. Why a stake, and not simply "more rounds"

`agent.tick` is 81% of decisions and 97% of cost in the measured five-day run.
Multiplying it by a round count to refine conversations where nothing is at stake
would be the cost of the idea with none of its content.

The multiscale literature converges on the same rule from several directions: the
heterogeneous multiscale method runs the micro solver only where the macro model
has no closure; Sunshine-Hill's perceptual simulation runs detail only where it
is observed; Broadie and colleagues allocate inner samples to scenarios near a
decision threshold. All three say: refine where the coarse answer is missing or
marginal, not everywhere.

Here "missing" is concrete. Three stakes, each already in the schema:

| Stake | Who can act | Reaches money by |
|---|---|---|
| `outage` | a Tallybird person present | escalation → earlier fix → earlier invoices |
| `invoice` | the debtor's bill-payer, present | a promise → pressure on `payment.timing` |
| `news` | nobody | `mention` → a notice → a ticket |

The invoice stake requires the *payer* to be in the room, not merely somebody
from the firm. A promise to pay from someone who cannot pay is a message they
might carry back, and scoring that as an obligation would make `kept` meaningless.

## 3. Rounds, and the one thing that makes them worth having

A round is one request per participant. Within the round the questions are
independent, which is what Jev supports; between rounds there is sequence, which
is what code supplies.

Everyone in a round reads the state as it stood when the round opened, and their
acts are folded in seat order afterwards. This is Ferber and Müller's
influence/reaction split, and it buys two things. Two people cannot both be the
first to press, so the fold is well defined. And nobody's decision depended on
somebody else's decision *within* the round, so the batch is genuinely
independent and can share cached calls.

The sequential dependence is the hypothesis, and it is visible in one place: the
rules twin will only promise once `pressed` is set, and `pressed` cannot be set
in the round that sets it. A one-shot encounter can never reach a promise.
`test_a_promise_needs_the_press_to_have_landed_first` is that statement as an
assertion.

### What is asked

| Ask | Mode | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `act` | P (sampled) | The verb. Options filtered by whether this person can act on the stake, so no mass lands on an option the world would discard. `other` always present: the ontology-gap signal. |
| `settled` | J (argmax) | Whether there is anything left to say. A judgement about the state, not a disposition. |
| `mood` | J | As `agent.tick`, written to `positions`. |
| `mention` | P | Only when this speaker holds live news somebody here lacks, and **only in the first round it is possible for them**. |

That last clause matters. Telling somebody something cannot be untold, so asking
each round would thin one propensity into near-certainty across three rounds —
the hazard-rate trap of DECIDE-0001, moved from across a day to inside a
conversation. Irreversible acts get asked once.

No round number reaches the model. Progress is described (`so_far`) rather than
counted: Jev is weak at numbers, and two tables at the same point in the same
kind of conversation should share one cached call.

## 4. Why nothing is written until it closes

Every effect happens once, in `_close`. A round that changed the room's mood has
changed nothing in the economy yet.

This is what keeps an episode atomic to the tick that spawned it. It reads a
world frozen at the start of the tick and writes once, so the tick stays a
barrier (CORE-0004) and no rollback is ever needed — conservative
synchronisation with one-tick lookahead, in the discrete-event sense. It also
means an episode cannot observe its own effects, which would be a causal loop
inside a single sim-instant.

The closing event is emitted first so that every effect can cite it. That is what
lets the existing recursive `causes` query walk from a conversation to an invoice
through an episode exactly as it already does through an encounter.

## 5. Double counting, and the other multi-resolution traps

Reynolds, Natrajan and Srinivasan name the failure modes that a two-resolution
model falls into, and each has an answer here:

- **Double counting.** The same meeting resolved at both resolutions would
  escalate twice. `episodes.run` returns every pair it consumed and `space` seeds
  its dedupe set with them, so the one-shot path skips them.
- **Chain disaggregation.** One refinement forcing its neighbours to refine.
  Depth is capped at one, in code and in the schema's `CHECK`.
- **Flip-flop.** A group forming and dissolving every tick. A two-sim-hour
  cooldown per person, which is Smallville's answer to the same problem.
- **Consistency between levels.** Davis and Bigelow's weak-consistency test:
  does the coarse model agree with the aggregate of the fine one? That is E2 in
  `ops/episodes.md`, and the first run says **no** — see §7.

## 6. The recursion, and why it is not decoration

A side conversation exists for a reason that falls out of another constraint. A
conversation seats four, so in a busy room the people at the next table have not
heard. News therefore has to ripple outward in a second, smaller conversation
rather than broadcasting to the zone — which would make the group cap
meaningless and diffusion instantaneous.

A side conversation is an episode with a parent and `depth = 1`: same rounds,
same gates, same fold-back. Over 21 sim-days, 10 of 64 episodes were side
conversations, and the seeded fact reached seven removes.

This was first shipped as the *only* recursion, on the grounds that inventing a
second trigger to show off depth would be speculative generality. WORLD-0008
adds exactly one more, and only the one the research pre-registered: a child
opens where the parent leaves a subset of its people with a stake of their own.
After an episode closes, a pair who have a *different* outage or bill between
them take it aside — the customer who has just cornered the vendor about an
outage and also owes the vendor's firm a bill. It opens after the close, so it
reads the world its parent wrote, and it is never about a matter an ancestor was
already about. News may now ripple one table further. Depth is capped at two, in
code and in the schema (migration 0010), and every child counts against the
daily ceiling.

## 7. What the first measurement says

From `ops/episodes.md`, five seeds, 21 sim-days per arm, on the rules twin:

- **E1, the economy.** Episodes cut total outage minutes from 2,703 to 2,100 on
  average, brought the first services invoice forward about three and a half
  hours, and produced 62 second-hand knowledge rows where the proxy produced
  none. Cost: +2.4% decisions.
- **E2, the docking.** Across the five seeds the proxy escalates 0.09–0.18 of
  the outage conversations it sees; episodes escalate 0.56–0.86, a mean gap of
  +0.51. **The two arms do not agree about what a meeting is worth.**

The first run of this harness, on one seed, reported sixteen hours, 1,575 → 1,335
minutes and 0.72. It was measuring a bug as well as the mechanism: the outage
stake counted a second Tallybird employee as somebody stuck on the outage, so
the vendor could press itself. `test_space` caught it the first time the default
world ran episodes; the stake now takes its askers from customers only
(WORLD-0007), and the figures above are after the fix.

E2 is the important result and it is not a comfortable one. If the cheap proxy
and the expensive arm disagree about base rates, then turning the resolution dial
changes the world as well as its fidelity, and any headline claim that compares
them is confounded.

Two caveats keep this from being over-read. The gap is an **upper bound**: with
episodes off the world keeps no record of a stake, so the proxy's denominator is
every conversation whose topic was the outage while the episode arm's is the
meetings *selected* into an episode — and selection needs a live stake, a free
cooldown and budget. Selection is inside the number along with resolution. And
both arms are the rules twin, so this measures what the mechanism does, not what
Jev does with it.

Decomposing the gap needs a third arm this harness does not run: episodes
computed at every eligible meeting and applied at none. That is the next piece of
work. Episodes first shipped switched off for this reason; since WORLD-0007 they
run in every world, and the gap is a base-rate change the default world carries.

## 8. What this costs, and what is still missing

- **On everywhere (WORLD-0007).** Strict replay needs `episode.round` answers in
  the golden cassette; recording them is `make fixture CALLS=record`, and it
  spends money.
- **The caps are judgements.** Three rounds, four participants, twelve a day
  (children included), depth two, two sim-hours of cooldown. Chosen mean so
  that a binding cap shows up in `exit_reason` rather than hiding; since
  WORLD-0008 the round cap ends 7% of episodes on rules and the daily ceiling
  binds on 7% of sim-days.
- **MEM-0002 is a third of MEM-0001.** No importance, retrieval, compaction or
  belief revision, and no retention policy. What it does record is now read:
  hearsay distance and promise records reach decisions (WORLD-0008). Mood is
  still written and not read by the next `agent.tick`.
- **Two fact topics.** A closed vocabulary cannot carry unanticipated news, which
  is the typed thesis' standing cost, measured as the ontology-gap rate rather
  than hidden.
- **No shadow arm**, which is what §7 needs.

## 9. What the live episodes showed, and what changed (WORLD-0008)

The first eight episodes in production, answered by live Jev, showed that three
rounds were mostly one round asked three times:

| measure (production, first eight episodes) | value |
|---|---:|
| round questions that were exact repeats | 12 of 64 |
| mean P(settled) | 0.13 |
| episodes ending settled | 0 of 8 |
| episodes ending on the round cap | 7 of 8 |
| escalation rate, episodes vs one-shot | 0.67 vs 0.08 |

A round's state carried only sticky flags, so whenever they had not moved the
next round was the same question, and its answer was a second draw of the same
propensity — a customer 62% likely to press became about 95% likely over three
rounds. And "has the matter been dealt with?" is never true of an outage that is
still down, so nobody ever finished.

WORLD-0008 makes four changes:

1. **Rounds remember.** Each round is told what everyone did in the round just
   gone and how long they have been at it.
2. **People end conversations.** Each person is asked whether they have had
   their say. A round in which everybody repeats their last act ends the episode
   as `stalled`.
3. **Two deep, by dependency** (§6).
4. **Memory is read back.** How someone heard of an outage reaches their first
   decision to report it; a payer's last kept or broken promise to a firm reaches
   the next conversation about a bill. Each is appended to the question only
   when there is something to say, so first-hand and first-time questions keep
   their bytes.

On the rules twin, over five seeds, 79% of episodes now end with everyone done,
8% stall, 6% empty and 7% hit the cap; 71 of 382 episodes are at depth one and
36 at depth two; outage minutes fall 24% against episodes off. The docking gap
barely moved on rules (+0.51 to +0.47), because most of it is structural — an
asker in an episode is offered `press` at a high base rate. The live gap is the
one round memory was built for, and it has to be re-measured once the cassette
is re-recorded and the change is deployed.
