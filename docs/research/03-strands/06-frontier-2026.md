## 1. Hierarchical / scene- or episode-based / nested LLM simulation

**EpisodeSim — "Classic AI Scaffolding for LLM Social Agents."** Anatole Gershman, arXiv:2609.01167, submitted 1 Sep 2026 [V]. Defines a *bounded social episode* (limited participant set, material setting, interaction frame, practical business, temporal progression, closure conditions) and implements it as a scene; a classical **World Master** holds authoritative episode state, validates proposed actions, tracks obligations/consequences and decides closure, with the LLM only generating role behaviour. *Mechanism:* episode state, obligations and closure live outside the text generator — exactly the sub-simulation contract jeve needs. *Evidence:* two settings (restaurant, hotel) × 3 runs, an 18-scene qualitative ablation; full system had 0 major script/agenda/closure failures vs 6 across minimal/grounded variants; author-judged, no benchmark. *Lesson:* copy the episode contract (roles, obligations, closure test) but treat the fidelity claim as unproven.

**TaskWeave — "Can LLM Agents Sustain Long-Horizon Organizational Dynamics?"** Zhu et al., arXiv:2606.01199, 31 May 2026; code ZhuXuanCH/TaskWeave (CAMEL-based) [V]. Formulates org simulation as a memory-centred coordination problem: a hierarchical framework with a Formulate–Partition–Diagnose–Align planning cycle and dependency-aware trace memory, evaluated in a year-long IT-company simulation against other multi-agent frameworks on organisational coherence, execution grounding and downstream enterprise-NLP utility. *Mechanism:* goals propagate down a role hierarchy; sub-task episodes write typed traces that later episodes depend on. *Evidence:* claims coherence over a simulated year and grounded artefacts; numbers not retrieved. *Lesson:* what folds back from a sub-simulation should be a typed trace with dependencies, not a summary.

**Concordia v2.3.0 (4 Feb 2026) / v2.4.0 (6 Mar 2026)**, PyPI gdm-concordia 2.4.0 [V]. Scenes are first-class: `SceneTypeSpec` (game master, default premise, `action_spec`, allowed participants) and `SceneSpec` (participants, `num_rounds`, start time); `SceneBasedTerminator` ends a scene by round count; scene-aware event delivery prevents leakage between simultaneous scenes; `physically_situated_and_dramaturgic`, `dialogic_and_dramaturgic`, `game_theoretic_and_dramaturgic` GM prefabs. A new `interrupt_driven` GM prefab schedules entities by timers and prefix-based interrupt masks and "skips dead air" in simulated time. `ActionSpec.OutputType` includes `FREE`, `CHOICE`, `FLOAT` (plus GM types `NEXT_GAME_MASTER`, `RESOLVE`, `TERMINATE`), and v2.4.0 parses choice/float answers via JSON. *Mechanism vs jeve:* `SceneSpec` = bounded participants + rounds + typed action spec, i.e. a ready-made sub-simulation shape; `NEXT_GAME_MASTER` hands control between rule-sets but **there is no primitive for a scene to spawn an inner simulation and return a result**. *Lesson:* reuse the SceneSpec vocabulary; nesting and fold-back must be jeve's own.

**AgentSociety 2** — arXiv:2607.11895; PyPI agentsociety2 2.9.0 released 17 Sep 2026 [V]. An integrated research environment coupling AI-social-scientist agents with the simulator; seven demonstrations span micro-level lab experiments, meso-level social-media dynamics and macro-level urban scenarios. Per DeepWiki, environment modules (e.g. `SimpleSocialSpace`) can bound a group; litellm role routing lets a `nano` model handle high-frequency operations; skills load "metadata-first, selected-only." No caching of identical situations and no prototype agents. *Lesson:* meso is treated as a *scenario scale*, not as a nested sub-simulation.

Also noted [V-t]: GROVE, "Simulating Organized Group Behavior: New Framework, Benchmark, and Analysis" (Zou et al., arXiv:2604.09874, Apr 2026; HF dataset jayzou3773/GROVE) — the group as unit of analysis with codified decision trees and temporal drift.

## 2. Cost-aware / mixed-fidelity generative ABM

**Topology-Aware LLM-Driven Social Simulation (MicroWorld).** Xu, Zhang, Zhou, Zeng, Lakshmanan, Ma; arXiv:2604.18011 (cs.SI/cs.DB), Apr 2026; code D2I-CUHKSZ/MicroWorld [V]. Converts event material into a graph + agent population, then runs the simulation with topology-aware clustering (threshold- or LLM-keyword-based), selective LLM invocation only for agents influenced in a step, PPR-guided directional influence and incremental memory. *Evidence:* cluster-based coordination "reduces token usage" with scale; PPR-guided runs track the reference trend more closely than baseline (numbers not extracted). *Lesson:* a group-level scheduler that decides *which* agents need a model call is the cheapest fidelity lever.

**GASim: A Graph-Accelerated Hybrid Framework for Social Simulation.** ACL 2026, arXiv:2605.07692; repo Jasmine0201/GASim [V]. An EDG coordinator partitions **core agents** (LLM with graph-based memory retrieval) from **ordinary agents** updated in parallel by a graph-attention model with fine-grained features. *Evidence:* 9.94× end-to-end speedup, <20% of baseline tokens vs a HiSim-style hybrid. *Lesson:* two-tier populations work; the ordinary tier need not be rules — a cheap learned propagator suffices.

**APS: Bias-Controlled Adaptive Prototype Simulation for Population-Scale LLM Agents.** arXiv:2605.27419, May 2026 (cs.MA) [V-t; authors not retrieved]. Scales from ~3k to 10M agents by simulating prototypes and shrinking individuals toward them with a population-dependent factor α(N), while "tail protection" routes isolated, heterogeneous or high-curvature individuals to direct LLM calls. *Evidence (from a third-party comparison table, not the paper):* ~381× reduction vs all-LLM with a measured fidelity cost of JSD ≈ 0.094. *Lesson:* archetypes plus an explicit escalation rule for outliers is the population-side twin of recursive micro-simulation, and the fidelity price should be measured as a divergence.

**Towards Agentic Agent-based Models: Feasibility, Performance, and Statistical Model Checking.** Blando, Guerrazzi, Porcedda, Squillace, Tschaikowski, Vandin; arXiv:2607.17948, 21 Jul 2026 [V]. Extends Mesa's Schelling model with a hybrid population where ordinary agents use the symbolic rule and one agent delegates neighbour classification to an LLM via tool calls; MultiVeStA statistical model checking estimates macro observables with confidence bounds. *Evidence:* smaller local models fail simple semantic classification or become "operationally unusable" during repeated tool-call generation; larger models pass. *Lesson:* SMC (multi-run, CI-bounded macro observables) is the right harness for "does the sub-simulation change anything?"

**Restoring Heterogeneity in LLM-based Social Simulation: An Audience Segmentation Approach.** Qin, Li, Cheng; arXiv:2604.06663, 8 Apr 2026 [V]. Six segmentation configurations × two open-weight LLMs on US climate-opinion data, scored on distributional, structural and predictive fidelity. *Evidence:* moderate enrichment helps, further identifier expansion can worsen structural/predictive fidelity; compact configurations often match or beat richer ones; no configuration dominates. *Lesson:* archetype cards for jeve's ~20 staff should be compact and validated per fidelity dimension.

Related [V]: *Do Personality-Tuned LLMs Make Better Social Agents?* (Krabbe & Shi, arXiv:2609.21857, 18 Sep 2026) — fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B/Ministral-8B were **not** better at personality role-play than their baselines. *Will Scaling Improve Social Simulation with LLMs?* (Ziems, Held, Karaca, Grusky, Hashimoto, Yang; arXiv:2607.02464, 2 Jul 2026) — 85 fixed-compute LLMs + 35 open models: opinion/behaviour tasks scale, but calibration to human biases (risk aversion, correlated-reward heuristics) does not, even after fine-tuning 0.5B→8B. *Lesson for both:* generative capability is not the bottleneck for behavioural fidelity, which weakens the case that a cheap typed engine is intrinsically lower-fidelity.

## 3. Multi-party / small-group episodes with structured outcomes

**From Simulated Citizens to Simulated Deliberation: Challenges in Representation and Interaction.** Chaemin Jang et al., arXiv:2609.07573, 7 Sep 2026 [V]. Census-grounded Korean personas debate real policy questions, benchmarked against national surveys. *Evidence:* persona responses are over-concentrated and often reverse demographic differences; **sealed-monologue agents change stance at similar rates and reach nearly the same final balance as full debates**; groups seeded with very different positions often converge regardless. *Lesson:* the strongest 2026 evidence bearing on the jeve hypothesis is negative — richer intra-encounter interaction did not move the outcome distribution. Any sub-simulation must be compared against a no-interaction null.

**Think-Before-Speak (TBS).** Kaiqi Yang, Tai-Quan Peng, Sanguk Lee, Hui Liu; arXiv:2606.03137, Jun 2026 (KDD'26 workshop) [V]. Interval-based framework: every interval each agent updates *structured internal states* (dissonance appraisal, perceived opinion climate, isolation risk, response strategy, willingness to speak); an orchestrator resolves competing intentions and commits one public utterance. *Evidence:* simulated town halls on a climate policy; dissonance raises willingness to speak, silence pressure lowers it; once intention forms, public expression is governed mostly by turn-allocation rules. *Lesson:* a group episode can be run as typed state updates plus one arbitration decision per interval — directly expressible as Jev questions; the utterance itself is optional.

**Belief Engine: Configurable and Inspectable Stance Dynamics in Multi-Agent LLM Deliberation.** Joshua C. Yang, Maurice Flechtner, Damian Dailisan, Michiel A. Bakker; arXiv:2605.15343, 18 May 2026 [V]. Belief is an evidential state exposed as a scalar stance, updated by a log-odds rule with evidence-uptake *u* and prior-anchoring *a*; arguments are extracted into structured memory. *Evidence:* parameter sweeps across base LLMs shape dynamics reliably; on the human DEBATE dataset BE best reconstructs participants whose final stance follows extracted evidence. *Lesson:* keep the group's "state" as typed scalars with explicit update rules; use language only for evidence extraction.

**From Process Loss to Assembly Bonus: Human-Grounded Diagnosis of Multi-Agent LLM Collaboration.** Ala N. Tak, Teruhisa Misu, Kumar Akash, Zhaobo K. Zheng, Kevin H. Joo, Jonathan Gratch; arXiv:2609.13261, 15 Sep 2026 [V]. Matches human group chats to LLM deliberation traces on Wason-style and other reasoning tasks. *Evidence:* same assembly-bonus asymmetry as humans, but LLM groups follow majorities more, surface less unique information and converge earlier; correct minority signals win only if re-expressed early; interventions give modest gains. *Lesson:* multi-round episodes need explicit dissent/turn mechanics or they collapse to the majority in round one.

**Loop-Back Authority in LLM Agent Teams.** Burak Agachan, Max van Duijn, Amirhossein Zohrehvand; arXiv:2609.14767, 13 Sep 2026 [V]. Paired flat vs hierarchical five-agent teams on a BI-reporting task (43 pairs, 86 runs), isolating the manager's right to force revisions. *Evidence:* flat wins on utility (d=0.42) and clarity (d=0.34); hierarchy adds 53% more hedging and 51.5% more tokens with no specification gain. *Lesson:* each extra round inside a sub-simulation must earn its cost.

**Rethinking Multi-Agent Collaboration: When More Is Less.** Yishuo Yuan et al., arXiv:2609.19759, 17–18 Sep 2026 [V]. Expanding the pool or deepening recursion does not consistently help; gains depend on task-dependency structure (sparse/independent → helps; tightly coupled sequential → single agent wins). *Lesson:* recursion depth in "recursive micro-simulation" should be conditional on dependency structure, not fixed.

Also [V-t]: FocusGen, simulated focus group of persona agents (arXiv:2608.28001); *Speak for Me* meeting-participation agents on AMI (arXiv:2609.03923); *Testing Interchangeability in LLM Agent Teams* (arXiv:2609.05279).

## 4. Organisations, firms, supply chains

**EconSimulacra: A Digital Twin Platform of Socio-Economic Systems Powered by LLM Agents.** Ryuji Hashimoto, Masahiro Kaneko, Kentaro Ueda, Takehiro Takayanagi, Kiyoshi Izumi; arXiv:2606.26883, Jun 2026; PyPI `econsimulacra` [V]. Household consumption, firm pricing, narrative diffusion over a social network and spatial mobility on a grid; LLM agents with persona/prompt/memory modules, **actions emitted as constrained structured outputs (Outlines)**, rule-based agents supported, vLLM parallelism. *Evidence:* README shows no validation numbers. *Lesson:* nearest published analogue of jeve's firm economy already forces typed actions; no intra-firm micro-episodes.

Also: *Optimizing Service Operations via LLM-Powered Multi-Agent Simulation* (arXiv:2604.04383, Apr 2026) [V-t]; *Dynamics of Cognitive Heterogeneity … Multi-Stage Supply Chains* (arXiv:2604.17220) [V-t]; *Simulating Teams with LLM Agents: Interactive 2D Environments* (Almutairi et al., arXiv:2510.08242) [V-t]; ERPBench enterprise decisions vs rule-based opponents (arXiv:2609.04667) [V-t]; *Simulating team dynamics with generative agents* (Annals of OR, DOI 10.1007/s10479-026-07398-1) [listing only]. None isolates micro-interactions as the driver of macro outcomes.

## 5. Typed / non-generative decision engines

**Should LLM Agents Decide in Social Simulations? Comparing Finite-State and LLM-Based Decision Policies.** López, Pastor-Galindo, Ruipérez-Valiente; arXiv:2606.12369, Jun 2026 [V]. A first-order Markov transition-matrix policy is the reference; LLaMA 3.1, GPT-OSS and Mistral 24B under base/guided/probabilistic prompting must reproduce it in a 1,000-agent synthetic social network over 10,000 decision steps (read/like/share/reply/post). *Evidence:* mean JSD 0.212 from the intended policy; LLM policies 135–1,337× slower (mean 563×); best case Mistral JSD 0.077; GPT-OSS ranged 0.035–0.672 depending on prompt. *Lesson:* the only 2026 head-to-head of explicit vs generative decision policies; typed/explicit engines win on cost and controllability, and any decision engine (including Jev) should be checked for behavioural fidelity against a known policy.

**Solver–Sampler Mismatch.** Sandro Andric, arXiv:2604.11840, 16 Apr 2026 [V]. In three negotiation environments, native reasoning models over-optimise (GPT-5.2 native ended in authority decisions 45/45 runs) while bounded reflection recovers compromise. *Lesson:* for sampling boundedly rational behaviour, weaker/cheaper decision processes can be *more* faithful.

**TypeSafe Jev and its ecosystem** [V]. Early access opened 15 Sep 2026 (MarkTechPost, 19 Sep; Vercel AI Gateway's fastest-adopted model by 18 Sep). Primitives per the community lists: **Noul** (probability a yes/no claim is true), **Choice** (2–255 options with per-option probabilities), **Score** (ordered rubric distribution); one parallel pass, 70–500 ms, $0.042/M input tokens, output free. Adjacent evidence: hermes-agent RFC #113008 (16 Sep 2026) defines a provider-agnostic `DecisionProvider` with BinaryQuestion/ChoiceQuestion/OrdinalQuestion and a **shadow-evaluation mode** for comparing decision backends before switching; issue #118599 (21 Sep) measured a single-token logprob decision at 754 ms vs 2,094 ms for the same decision as JSON prose. Simulation uses are demos, not papers: **Jevtown** (10,000 personas reacting to posts via `Score` on 60 traits and batched `Choice` reactions), Crowdcheck (persona simulation), typesafe-mario (Choice/Noul/Score every 8 frames from structured emulator state). Caveat from an independent audit cited in AbdelStark/awesome-typesafe-jev: with an "unknown" option Jev chose it for 95% of ambiguous items; forced-choice accuracy on that set was 0%. *Jev-Mem* (Jiang, Li, Li; arXiv:2609.23986, 21 Sep 2026) is a "System-One-controlled" memory plane (fixed type/relation/budget/stopping rules; 0.777 LLM-judge on LoCoMo, −36.7% latency) — the pattern, not Jev. *Lesson:* run jeve's sub-simulation in shadow mode against the current dyadic engine, and always give Jev an abstain option that the sub-simulation handles explicitly.

## 6. Validation work

- **Larooij & Törnberg** — arXiv:2504.03274 (Apr 2025), published as "Validation is the central challenge for generative social simulation," *Artificial Intelligence Review*, DOI 10.1007/s10462-025-11412-6 [V-listing]; 22 of 35 surveyed studies used believability as primary validation.
- **TRAILS** — Ye, Cao, Chen, Ferrara; arXiv:2605.18890, 20 May 2026 [V]. Persona-format/instruction perturbations shift cooperation up to 76 pp in one frontier model and 1 pp in another; taxonomy at agent (micro), interaction (meso) and system (macro) levels. *Lesson:* audit the sub-simulation's meso parameters (rounds, order, clock) per claim and per model.
- **Mechanism Plausibility Scale** — Zhao, Pham, Vincent; arXiv:2605.12824, 14 May 2026 [V]. Four-level scale separating generative sufficiency from mechanistic plausibility. *Lesson:* say which level the sub-simulation claims.
- **Hullman, Broska, Sun, Shaw** — arXiv:2602.15785, 18 Feb 2026 [V]. Heuristic repair vs statistical calibration with auxiliary human data; calibration preserves validity at lower cost. *Lesson:* calibrate fold-back quantities against a small ground-truth sample instead of prompt-tuning.
- **Tomašević et al.** — *Towards Operational Validation … Reddit-like Technology Forum*, arXiv:2508.21740 (v3) [V-t]: machine–machine baseline, empirical calibration, 30-run uncertainty across activity, network, toxicity, topic and style.
- **Position: AI Agents Are Not (Yet) a Panacea for Social Simulation** — arXiv:2603.00113, Mar 2026 [V-t]: collective outcomes are mediated by agent–environment co-dynamics, exposure, scheduling and initial priors; proposes an environment-involved POMG with explicit scheduler and visibility function. *Lesson:* jeve's tick scheduler and co-location rule may dominate outcomes more than the encounter engine.
- **Reason-Mediated Behavioral Models** — Pandey & Jajoo, arXiv:2607.24649, 28 Jul 2026 [V]: LLM-simulated reasons are brittle even when outcomes match. **Silicon Society Cookbook** — Bück-Kaeffer et al., arXiv:2605.00197, 4 May 2026 [V]: base-model choice is the single most influential design variable.

## (a) Three most relevant items, ranked

1. **Jang et al., 2609.07573** — because it is direct, recent, human-benchmarked evidence that adding interaction inside an episode did not change the outcome distribution. It defines the null jeve must beat.
2. **López et al., 2606.12369** — because it is the only controlled comparison of explicit/typed decision policies against generative ones inside a running social simulation, with fidelity (JSD) and cost (563×) numbers; it is the template for a Jev-vs-prose benchmark.
3. **Concordia v2.4 scenes + EpisodeSim (2609.01167)** — jointly, because they give the concrete data structure (participants, rounds, typed action spec, closure rule, authoritative episode state) for a bounded sub-simulation; neither nests or folds back, so that is the open design.

## (b) Searched for and did NOT find

- No 2025–2026 paper implementing **recursive or nested sub-simulations** (a macro simulation spawning bounded inner simulations and folding typed results back). Concordia scenes and EpisodeSim are single-level.
- No paper using a **typed-only, non-generative decision model** (Jev or logprob/enum-constrained LLM) as the engine of a social or economic simulation, and **no benchmark of typed agents vs prose agents for simulation fidelity**. Jevtown is an unpublished demo; the hermes DecisionProvider is agent infrastructure.
- No 2026 paper on **caching identical agent situations** in a population simulation (only generic agentic plan caching, arXiv:2506.14852 [M]); APS shares prototypes, it does not cache.
- No explicit **escalation policy** paper (when to route an encounter from a dyadic step to a richer multi-round episode); the nearest are APS's tail protection and GASim's core/ordinary split, both population-level.
- No study that **varies intra-encounter resolution** (rounds, clock granularity) and measures macro change; the only related evidence is negative (2609.07573) or sensitivity-based (TRAILS).
- No AAMAS 2026, JASSS 2026 or ICLR/ICML 2026 main-track paper on hierarchical LLM social simulation surfaced (AAMAS proceedings and JASSS were unreachable; JASSS 29(1) GPLab exists but was not read).
- Concordia's `interrupt_driven` GM exists in the repo but appears in no changelog or release note, so its introduction date could not be confirmed.
