## 1. "Recursive simulation" as a term of art

**Gilmer & Sullivan (WSC 1998–2005).** Recursive simulation is "a simulation, or an entity in the simulation, creating another instance of the same simulation, running it and using its results" [V, abstract]. In their *eaglet* combat prototype, simulated command elements evaluate each candidate order by running a child copy forward and comparing outcomes; they report that data collected "indicates that the quality of decisionmaking by the simulated command elements is improved" [V]. Multitrajectory simulation (1998) is the sibling technique: instead of drawing one branch at a random event, the run *splits* into weighted trajectories, managed by a class library that hides the bookkeeping [V]. The later "event analysis" paper addresses the problem that child-sim events pollute parent event logs [V exists; details [M]]. Internals I could not verify: recursion depth used, child RNG seeding, horizon bounding [M].
*Lesson:* Gilmer's recursion is strictly decision-time; nothing simulated in the child becomes a real event. jeve's proposal is execution-time (the sub-sim *is* the encounter). Both need Gilmer's discipline of tagging hypothetical events distinctly.

**Cloning (Hybinette & Fujimoto 2001)** is the PDES formalisation: clone the running simulation at a decision point, explore alternative futures in parallel, and use *virtual logical processes* so computation common to clones is not repeated [V].
*Lesson:* sibling sub-sims that diverge late should share their common prefix — this is the structural justification for memoising sub-sim prefixes.

**Anticipatory systems.** Rosen's definition (a system with a predictive model of itself, whose present state changes in accord with the model's prediction) [V] is the philosophical root; Davidsson's ALQAAA agents [V] are the practical version: the Anticipator runs one-step predictions in a world model *in which all other agents are represented only by their reactive layer*, and edits the Reactor when it foresees an undesired state. I-POMDPs [V] formalise nested modelling of others to a finite depth.
*Lesson:* a sub-sim may model the *other* participants at lower fidelity than the spawning agent; and nesting depth must be a hard parameter, not emergent.

**Nested Monte-Carlo Search (Cazenave 2009)** is the cleanest algorithmic "recursive simulation": a level-n search evaluates each move by a level-(n−1) search, down to random playouts at level 0, memorising the best sequence found [V]. Cost is geometric in level; in practice levels 2–4 are used [M]. NRPA (Rosin 2011) adapts the playout policy across levels [V]. A 2025 paper embeds LLMs in NRPA for dialogue planning (arXiv 2511.21706 [V url]).
*Lesson:* the useful recursion depth is small and the benefit comes from *adapting the cheap policy* between levels — i.e., using sub-sim results to recalibrate the one-shot model.

**Nested Monte Carlo in finance (Gordy & Juneja 2010; Broadie et al. 2011; Giles & Haji-Ali 2019).** The outer loop draws scenarios, the inner loop simulates within each. Gordy & Juneja show the inner estimate contributes *bias* of order 1/N_inner and the outer loop *variance* of order 1/N_outer; for a fixed budget the optimum grows the outer count faster (N_outer ∝ budget^{2/3}, N_inner ∝ budget^{1/3} [M]) and "a relatively small number of trials in the inner step can yield accurate estimates" [V]. Broadie et al. allocate inner effort *sequentially* to scenarios where the marginal change of the risk estimator is largest — scenarios near the loss threshold — and prove a faster convergence order than uniform allocation [V].
*Lesson:* this is the most rigorous existing answer to "when is a sub-simulation worth it": spend micro-rounds only where the situation sits near a decision boundary, and prefer many distinct situations with few rounds over few situations with many rounds.

**SMC²** (Chopin et al. 2013 [M]) is the inference-side analogue: particle filters nested inside an SMC sampler over parameters. Symbiotic simulation (Aydt et al. 2008/09 [V]) runs what-if sims alongside a live system to choose control actions — again decision-time.

## 2. LLM agents that simulate before acting

Verified by reading the released code (DeepWiki):

- **Hypothetical Minds** [V]: no rollouts. The ToM module writes NL hypotheses about the opponent's strategy, predicts the opponent's next "inventory", compares with the observed one, and updates each hypothesis's value by a Rescorla–Wagner rule; hypotheses above a threshold are "validated". Gains on Melting Pot over LLM-agent and RL baselines are attributed to hypothesis evaluation/refinement [V].
- **RAP** [V]: MCTS in which the LLM predicts the next state from (state, action) text; reward mixes LLM log-likelihood of the action ("intuition"), a self-evaluation score, and goal satisfaction; final answer is the max-reward trace.
- **LLM-MCTS** [V]: LLM supplies a commonsense prior over object locations (world model) and an action-sampling policy heuristic inside MCTS; outperforms MCTS-only and LLM-policy-only baselines [V, abstract].
- **GDP-Zero** [V]: open-loop MCTS; per real turn, 20 simulated dialogues (default) where the LLM plays user simulator, system model, prior and value; the actual utterance is the best-valued *realisation* found during search; ablating response selection hurts. Preferred over ChatGPT up to 59% in PersuasionForGood [V].
- **Generative Agents** [V]: conversations are generated utterance-by-utterance (≤8 turns) with retrieval + relationship summary; no lookahead, no simulated alternatives.
- Related but unverified: SimToM (arXiv 2311.10227 [M]) — two-stage perspective-taking, no simulation; Wang et al. "Can LMs serve as text-based world simulators?" (arXiv 2406.06485 [M]) — reports LLM state-transition accuracy is poor for environment-driven and arithmetic transitions; ToMAgent (arXiv 2509.22887 [V url]) uses simulated remaining-dialogue rollouts as reward for DPO.

**Decision-time vs execution-time recursion.** Every LLM paper above is decision-time: the simulated future is a *search heuristic*, discarded after the choice, and its cost is paid per real action (GDP-Zero: ~20 simulated conversations per real turn). jeve's proposal is execution-time: the sub-sim is the canonical account of what happened, its outcome is committed, and it is run once per situation. That difference changes everything downstream: execution-time sub-sims must be deterministic, cacheable and causally tagged; decision-time ones need not be. The LLM evidence also cautions that generative world models drift (Wang et al. [M]); jeve's typed decisions avoid the free-text state problem entirely, which is the concrete reason the "nearly free and cacheable" hypothesis is plausible.

## 3. Multi-fidelity and adaptive fidelity

**MFMC (Peherstorfer et al. 2016/2018)** [V]: low-fidelity models act as control variates for a high-fidelity estimator. From memory [M]: with models ordered by decreasing squared correlation ρ_i² with the high-fidelity output and costs w_i, the optimal evaluation ratios are r_i = sqrt(w_1(ρ_i² − ρ_{i+1}²) / (w_i(1 − ρ_2²))), valid when w_{i−1}/w_i > (ρ_{i−1}² − ρ_i²)/(ρ_i² − ρ_{i+1}²); for two models the variance at equal cost is (sqrt(1 − ρ²) + sqrt(w ρ²))² times plain MC, so the cheap model only helps when that factor is < 1 — i.e., high correlation *and* low relative cost.
**MLMC (Giles 2008)** [V]: telescoping E[P_L] = E[P_0] + Σ E[P_ℓ − P_{ℓ−1}], with each difference computed on *coupled* samples (same random inputs at both levels) so its variance shrinks with level; samples per level ∝ sqrt(V_ℓ / C_ℓ) [M].
**Hybrid ABM/ODE switching (Bobashev et al. 2007)** [V]: run the ABM while infected count is below a threshold (where stochasticity and local structure matter), switch to compartmental above it, using ABM aggregates as ODE initial conditions; justified by the diminishing informational value of individual-level detail as counts grow. A 2025 JASSS paper (28(1)5 [V url]) generalises this coupling.
**Multi-level ABM (Morvan 2012; Picault, Mathieu, Morvan)** [V]: dynamic level of detail (JAM-FREE traffic, IRM4MLS) — agents are aggregated into macro-agents where detail is unnecessary and disaggregated where it matters, with influence/reaction to keep levels consistent. This is the closest ABM precedent, but every instance I found refines *spatially* (a road segment, a crowd) and switches representation of *the same* entities, not the fidelity of an *interaction*.
*Lessons:* (i) the MFMC/MLMC condition is a computable test: pilot-run a sample of encounters both one-shot and micro, estimate ρ and w, and only adopt micro-sims for encounter classes where the variance factor is < 1; (ii) the MLMC coupling trick requires the one-shot and the micro-sim to consume the *same* keyed random draws, which jeve's seed-by-identity design already permits; (iii) Bobashev's threshold rule is the right *shape* for a per-instance switch.

## 4. Memoisation, surrogates, deterministic seeding

**Aggregate models as learned summaries of micro-sims.** Military MRM calibrates Lanchester attrition coefficients from high-resolution DES output (arXiv 2607.15312 [V url]); Davis & Hillestad 1993 [V] and RAND MR-1004/MR-1750 [V] frame this as "families of models" where the low-resolution member is calibrated to, and validated against, the high-resolution one. Reynolds et al. [V] show naive aggregation/disaggregation causes *chain disaggregation* (one disaggregation forces its neighbours to disaggregate) and propose Multiple Resolution Entities that keep both representations consistent.
**Emulators.** Kennedy & O'Hagan [V] (GP emulator + discrepancy term); Lamperti et al. [V] (ML surrogate of ABM input→output with iterative sampling, tested on Brock–Hommes and the Islands model).
**Canonicalisation.** Transposition tables with Zobrist hashing [M] are the standard mechanism for recognising that two search paths reached the same position; no ABM paper I found applies this to sub-simulations.
**Seeding.** Random123 [V, docs read]: output = keyed bijection f(key, counter); "the same (counter, key) tuple always produces the same result", so generation is independent of execution order and parallel decomposition; docs recommend loop/entity-dependent variables as counters. JAX JEP 263 [V, docs read]: Threefry counter PRNG + functional splitting; explicit goal "reproducibility regardless of backend" by avoiding global sequential state; `fold_in(key, id)` derives "a per-step or per-example key without carrying any key-threading through your loop", each derived from the parent, not its predecessor. Claessen & Pałka [V] and Steele et al. [V] give the splittable-PRNG theory (value determined by the split path). L'Ecuyer's streams/substreams [V] are the OR-community equivalent. Contrast: Mesa uses one model-level `random.Random`/`Generator` shared by all agents [V, docs read], so adding an agent perturbs every later draw.
*Lessons:* jeve's "seed by what the draw is about" is already the Random123/JAX idiom; the sub-sim key should be `fold_in(encounter_key, (round, question_id))` and a sub-sub-sim `fold_in(sub_key, child_id)`. Cache the sub-sim by the hash of (canonicalised situation, question set, key), exactly as Jev's (state, questions) cache works today.

## 5. Validation of multi-level models

Grimm et al. 2005 [V]: pattern-oriented modelling uses *multiple* observed patterns at *multiple* scales to reject model structures and to find the resolution "sufficient" to reproduce them; Grimm & Railsback 2012 extend this as a "multi-scope" [M]. Bigelow & Davis MR-1750 [V] argue that validation of MRMPM models must be question-specific and that higher resolution is not automatically more valid; MR-1570 [V] proposes "motivated metamodels" (statistical metamodels constrained by causal reasoning) as the low-resolution member. Null-model testing for emergence (Gotelli–Graves tradition) [M] is the analogue of comparing micro-sim outcomes with a shuffled/one-shot baseline.
*Lesson:* the acceptance test for micro-sims is not "more detail" but "macro patterns (turnover, sales, morale distributions, encounter-outcome distributions) that the one-shot model cannot reproduce and the micro-sim can, at more than one scale."

## 6. Time handling for nested sims

DEVS closure under coupling [V]: a coupled model is externally an atomic model, so a sub-sim with a private fine clock is legitimate provided its outputs are emitted at a macro event time. Co-simulation master algorithms [M] name the pattern: micro-steps inside a macro communication step; Jacobi (all units read the step-start state, outputs applied together) vs Gauss–Seidel (sequential). Ferber & Müller [V] supply the fold-back semantics: agents (here, sub-sims) emit *influences*; a single *reaction* computes the next state from all influences, so simultaneous outcomes cannot double-apply. Fujimoto/Jefferson [V]: if every sub-sim reads only the tick-start snapshot and writes only into the reaction phase, lookahead is one tick and the system is conservative — no rollback is ever required; Time Warp becomes necessary only if sub-sims may read each other's in-progress state.

## (a) When to spawn a micro-simulation

Best-justified rule, composed from Broadie et al. (near-threshold allocation), Bobashev (threshold switch), MFMC (variance test) and Davis/Bigelow (question-driven resolution):

1. **Class gate (offline, MFMC).** For each encounter class, pilot-run N encounters both ways with identical keys; estimate ρ between one-shot and micro outcomes and cost ratio w. Enable micro-sims for the class only if (sqrt(1−ρ²) + sqrt(wρ²))² < 1 [M formula] *and* POM-style macro patterns differ.
2. **Instance gate (online, Broadie/Bobashev).** Spawn when the one-shot typed decision is near its boundary (boolean probability within ε of 0.5; enum top-2 gap small; numeric scale near a threshold that changes downstream branching) *and* the encounter's causal id feeds a macro state variable (hire/fire, sale, escalation).
3. **Budget (Gordy–Juneja).** Fix rounds per micro-sim small; spend the budget on the number of distinct near-threshold situations.
4. **Depth (I-POMDP/NMCS/Reynolds).** Hard depth ≤ 2, group size cap, and no chain spawning: a sub-sim may spawn a child only under gate 2 applied to its own decisions.

## (b) Deterministic seeding, caching, replay

Counter-based RNG keyed by identity (Random123/JAX [V]): `key_sub = fold_in(key_encounter, sub_sim_id)`, `draw = f(key_sub, (round, question_id))`. Results are then independent of execution order and parallelism, so sub-sims can run concurrently and replay byte-identically. Cache entry = hash(canonical situation, question set, key) → typed outcome + causal id; canonicalise by relabelling participants to role slots and quantising numerics to Jev's own buckets (transposition-table logic [M]). Store only Jev responses and the folded outcome; the internal trace is reconstructible from the key, with an optional trace log keyed by encounter id for Gilmer-style event analysis.

## (c) Keeping sub-sim time consistent with the tick barrier

Treat each micro-sim as an atomic DEVS component with a private round counter (closure under coupling [V]); Jacobi scheduling: all sub-sims of a tick read the tick-start snapshot; their outcomes are *influences* combined once in a reaction phase ordered by causal id (Ferber & Müller [V]); no decision inside the same tick may read a sub-sim's outcome; if a micro-sim spans macro time (a meeting longer than 15 min), schedule its output event at the later tick and mark participants busy. This is conservative synchronisation with one-tick lookahead — no rollback, no double counting, no causal loop.

## (d) Absent from the literature

- No work found that forms *ad hoc groups at execution time* and runs a bounded higher-resolution sub-simulation of their *interaction*, folding a committed outcome back — multi-level ABM refines/coarsens *entities*, MRM disaggregates *attributes*, and recursive/cloned simulation is decision-time only.
- No per-instance decision rule for one-shot vs multi-round interaction fidelity; nested-simulation and MFMC allocation rules are population-level (Broadie's sequential allocation and Bobashev's threshold are the nearest).
- No memoised sub-simulation keyed by canonical situation with path-seeded RNG in ABM (transposition tables exist only in game search).
- No evidence that simulated multi-round interactions improve *aggregate* believability or calibration of a social/economic ABM versus one-shot decisions; LLM-rollout papers measure task success, and Wang et al. [M] warn that generative world models are unreliable state simulators — which is precisely the gap typed, cacheable micro-decisions could fill.
- No published cross-scale validation of nested micro-sims (POM supplies the method; nobody has applied it).
