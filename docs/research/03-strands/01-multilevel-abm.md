## Notes by strand

**Multi-level ABM (French school).** Morvan's survey [1] and the four-patterns paper [4] frame the field; IRM4MLS [2] is the formal core: each level has its own state, time step and *reaction* function, agents emit *influences* into the levels they may influence, and the level decides what is committed. Morvan & Kubera [3] then show that most ML-ABM engines mishandle time, the concrete failure being a level reading another level's state mid-update. PADAWAN [6] is the most permissive meta-model: "any agent may dynamically encapsulate an environment… this structure must be able to change in time", "any agent may be situated in several environments at the same time", and "there should be no limiting principle to the creation or dissolution of any level". Each environment carries its own interaction matrix and period. GAMA [7] made the same idea operational with `capture`/`release`: a building captures the people inside it, changes their species, runs their behaviour itself, and releases them when *it* decides. Gil-Quijano et al. [5] show the other trigger, detecting groups by clustering and reifying them; their published lesson is that validation of such dynamic models is the weak point.

**Co-simulation / DEVS.** Camus et al. [8] treat micro and macro as two models coordinated by agents through *coupling artifacts*, with dedicated *upward/downward* artifacts for resolution change and DEVS-style conservative synchronisation; with a 4:1 tick ratio the macro simply takes the last micro output, an explicit (and lossy) sampling choice. ML-DEVS [9] formalises downward *event activation* and upward information propagation.

**Models within models.** LevelSpace [10] is the closest engineering precedent. Child models are agents; the parent can only receive strings, numbers and lists back; relationships are *synchronous* (constant tick ratio) or *asynchronous* (parent pauses). Their "Wolf Chases Sheep" example is structurally your proposal: an encounter in the coarse model spawns a child model at finer time and space, the parent pauses, and a typed outcome (caught / escaped) is returned. Head et al. [11] add the cognitive variant with two governing knobs: number and duration of micro-simulations. Swarm [12] already allowed nested swarms with their own schedules, created and destroyed at run time, and agents using swarms as internal models. Repast's "recursive" [13] is a philosophy of porous actors plus nested `Context` containers; there is no nested-clock sub-simulation primitive.

**Holonic MAS.** Gaud et al. [14] dynamically merge pedestrians with the same goal into super-holons with a coarser behaviour and a *scheduling holarchy*; energy-like indicators of accuracy deviation decide when to split. Direction of travel is cost reduction (meso is cheaper, confirmed), not fidelity gain. Rodriguez et al. define the three holon structures (federation, moderated group, fusion) and the CRIO/Janus tooling; Gerber et al. give formation and reconfiguration algorithms.

**Defense MRM.** Davis & Hillestad [15] coined the "families of models" problem and warned that cross-resolution connection is easy only at the software-plumbing level; Davis & Bigelow define weak/strong consistency as commutation of "simulate" and "aggregate/disaggregate" and treat calibration of the aggregate model against the detailed one as the central difficulty. Reynolds et al. [16] argue that aggregation/disaggregation *itself* is the problem (chain disaggregation, flip-flop, transition cost, concurrent interactions at two levels whose effects must be combined) and instead keep every representation live (MRE) with a dependency graph and mapping functions. Rabelo et al. [17] report the same three unsolved problems in 2025. OneSAF/ModSAF are entity-level with aggregate units to brigade (MITRE, Wittman) [V]; I did not obtain JWARS-specific mechanism detail.

**Dynamic LOD.** Navarro et al. [18] is the best-measured ABM LOD paper: LOD is a run-time parameter, agents far from focus points are aggregated (psychological attributes only), and the paper reports CPU gain against *dissimilarity* to a full-resolution run. Games LOD [19] governs detail by proximity to the player; Osborne & Dickinson use collapsible group trees, i.e. a group is a node that expands into individuals on demand.

**Hybrid/multi-method.** Brailsford et al. [20] catalogue ABM+SD+DES hybrids and their integration issues. Burghout et al. [21] give the cleanest statement of what "consistent at the boundary" means when a micro region sits inside a meso network. Micro–macro linkage in CGE-microsimulation (top-down/bottom-up iteration; Bourguignon et al. 2008) [M] and MATSim–SUMO couplings [M] are the economics/transport analogues.

**Physics multiscale.** HMM [22] runs the micro solver only where the macro model lacks a closure; equation-free [23] defines the *coarse time-stepper* (lift → short burst → restrict) and *projective integration*; gap-tooth/patch dynamics run micro only in small patches and interpolate, later applied directly to ABMs. AMR is the geometric analogue [M].

**Theory.** Simon [24]: in nearly decomposable systems intra-subsystem interaction is high-frequency and inter-subsystem interaction low-frequency, so the short-run behaviour of a subsystem is approximately independent of the rest, and the long-run effect on the rest is only aggregate. Sugarscape's trade rule [25] is the earliest widely-used example of a multi-round dyadic sub-process whose only output is a typed transfer.

## (a) Closest precedent

LevelSpace's *Wolf Chases Sheep* (Hjorth et al. 2020) is the direct structural precedent: on-demand child model at finer temporal and spatial resolution, triggered by a co-location event, parent paused, only typed values crossing back. PADAWAN supplies the formal generalisation you need for *recursion* (an environment nested in any agent, agents in several environments, unrestricted creation/dissolution of levels, per-environment period). GAMA's `capture`/`release` is the same for *host-controlled* groups. Equation-free computation is the closest *theory* of "spawn a short micro burst and restrict the result back", and it names the hard operator: lifting. Nothing found combines all four with a decision model as the micro dynamics.

## (b) What the literature says is hard

1. **Consistency and double counting.** Reynolds: attributes of the same entity at two levels lose correlation even when each level is valid; effects of *concurrent* interactions at both levels must be combined once. For jeve: while a sub-sim runs, the macro tick's own single-shot encounter for those people must be suppressed, and macro writes must be a pure function of the sub-sim's typed outcome. Davis's weak-consistency test is the measurable version: does "sub-sim then fold back" agree, in distribution, with "single-shot encounter"?
2. **Time.** Morvan & Kubera: never let one level read another in a transitory state. LevelSpace: pausing the parent (asynchronous) is legitimate; the price is that nothing outside the group may change during the sub-sim, so a sub-sim longer than one macro tick is only valid if Simon's short-run independence holds. Camus: choose explicitly which micro samples the macro sees.
3. **Thrashing and chain reactions.** Reynolds' flip-flop and chain disaggregation map onto groups that form/dissolve every tick and onto adjacent groups forced into sub-sims by each other (two cafe tables). Hysteresis on the formation criterion and a hard cap on depth are the standard remedies; Navarro's aggregation overhead (5–9%) shows bookkeeping cost is real but bounded.
4. **Lifting / aggregation error.** Constructing a group's local state from the macro state is under-determined (Davis: "disaggregate and lose info"); equation-free treats the lifting operator as the design decision.
5. **Validation.** Gil-Quijano and Morvan both flag dynamic-LOD models as hard to validate; Navarro's dissimilarity-to-reference is the only quantitative template found. Cost governance is done by indicators (holonic energy deviation, LevelSpace's count/duration knobs).

## (c) What appears absent

- Zooming *in* for **fidelity of social interaction**. Nearly all dynamic-resolution work zooms *out* for cost (holons, LOD, MRM); the fidelity-seeking direction has one toy example (chase) and no fidelity measurements.
- **Small ad hoc groups (2–6)** formed by co-location *and* co-involvement, with hysteresis, at depth > 1. PADAWAN and Swarm permit it in principle; no published run demonstrates a meeting spawning a side conversation.
- **Typed-only, decision-model-answered micro dynamics** with per-query cost accounting and (state, question-set) caching. LevelSpace's typed boundary is the nearest, but its micro dynamics are code, not a billed oracle.
- **Deterministic replay and causal provenance across levels.** DEVS gives causal ordering; none of the ABM work carries event ids from child to parent or promises byte-identical replay of spawned sub-sims.
- **Empirical evidence that sub-simulation improves macro validity**, as opposed to CPU cost. Davis's consistency measure and Navarro's dissimilarity are the instruments; nobody has pointed them at the fidelity-gain question.
