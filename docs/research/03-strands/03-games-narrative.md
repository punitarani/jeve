## 1. Simulation LOD for virtual humans

**Brom, Šerý, Poch, "Simulation Level of Detail for Virtual Humans", IVA 2007, LNAI 4722 pp. 1–14, DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-74997-4_1 [V].** Abstract [V]: LOD algorithms "for simplifying virtual space and behavior of virtual humans", built for "a large virtual-storytelling game populated by tens of complex virtual humans". Precursor: Šerý, Poch, Šafrata, Brom, "Level-of-Detail in Behaviour of Virtual Humans", SOFSEM 2006, DOI 10.1007/11611257_55 [V link]; follow-up Brom, Poch, Šerý, "Level of Detail AI for Virtual Characters in Games and Simulation", MIG 2010, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-16958-8_20 [V link]. Mechanism [M]: space is a tree; a *membrane* separates the fully simulated region (near the user or plot-important places) from coarser zones; outside it agents execute higher nodes of their behaviour hierarchy ("be at work" rather than "pick up tool"), and their state is expanded (disaggregated) when the membrane sweeps over them. Evidence: feasibility of the game, not a perceptual study [M]. **Lesson:** LOD must apply to *both* place and behaviour; the expensive part is the disaggregation on entry, so keep enough abstract state to expand plausibly.

**Sunshine-Hill & Badler, "Perceptually Realistic Behavior through Alibi Generation", AIIDE 2010, 6(1):83–88, https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12389 [V].** Abstract [V]: designers build "a highly realistic simulation, from which a perceptually identical 'perceptual simulation' is generated"; it "simulates only a small portion of the world at a time, and does so with inexpensive approximations", yet "it can be statistically guaranteed that the results are perceptually indistinguishable from those of the original simulation." Pedestrians the player watches get "increasingly more detailed plans … so it looks like they know what they're doing" [V]. **Lesson:** the strongest theoretical frame for jeve: the macro sim is the prior; a sub-sim is a *conditional sample* run only when observed/consequential; correctness is defined distributionally, not per event.

**Navarro, Flacher, Corruble, AAMAS 2011 pp. 701–708 [V].** "Simulate areas of high level of interest more precisely using microscopic models and other areas less precisely but more efficiently using macroscopic models"; framework includes "dynamic change of representation and navigation between models" [V]. **Lesson:** make "level of interest" an explicit scalar per region and treat the representation switch as the design core.

**O'Sullivan et al., "Levels of detail for crowds and groups", CGF 21(4):733–742, 2002 [V]**; **Osborne & Dickinson, AISB 2010, http://eprints.lincoln.ac.uk/2237/ [V]**: "Each group comprises individual agents organized using a hierarchical tree structure, and expanding and collapsing nodes … allows the efficient dynamic abstraction of individuals, depending on their proximity to the player." O'Sullivan includes *conversation* as an LOD dimension [M on detail]. **Lesson:** the *group* is the natural unit of collapse/expand; O&D's groups are static, jeve's would be ad hoc.

**Shipped AI-LOD:** KCD I/II GDC session "Supporting Thousands of NPCs…" ("AI level of detail simulation", ~2,400 NPCs) [V]; Watch Dogs: Legion "Census" (GDC 2021): "dynamically-generated schedules that span the entire open world, including meetings with friends, relations, and adversaries" [V]; Hitman 2016 crowd system (>1,000 crowd NPCs) [V]; Shadows of Doubt: "AI's update rate will only be high when nearest the player and when not doing actions that require them to be more aware" [V]; S.T.A.L.K.E.R. A-Life: offline NPCs "still invisibly going around on an overlayed world, going from point to point, hunting mutants, trading", returning online "with health and equipment having changed" [V]. **Lesson:** production systems use *continuous* rate/detail scaling, not discrete episodes; A-Life's known artefact — fights "start closer to you" at the switch boundary — is the canonical layer-seam bug.

## 2. Strategic/tactical two-layer games

**Total War [V community wiki/forums].** Autoresolve "compare[s] the two armies' relative power"; units carry a numeric "Combat Potential" including spells/buffs. Documented divergences: it "tends to destroy whole units rather than result in evenly distributed casualties", "does not account for units that require manual control", and "over values certain units and races" [V]. No public calibration procedure exists; CA re-tunes per patch [M]. **Lesson:** a proxy that matches win-probability but not the *shape* of outcomes (casualty distribution) is what players exploit and what would bias jeve's economy.

**Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord [V, community reverse-engineering].** "Send troops": random troop from each party, one attacks; `TroopPower=(2+Tier)(10+Tier)·0.02` (×1.2 mounted); `Damage=(0.5+0.5·rand)·40·(Pa/Pv)^0.7·Perk`; dead/wounded if `rand(0..MaxHP)<Damage`; "defenders have a massive hidden defensive bonus" [V]. The same loop resolves every NPC-vs-NPC battle on the map [M]. Modders ship "Fixed Simulations", "Auto Resolve Rebalanced" [V]. **Lesson:** a *round-based loop of typed pairwise decisions* is already a micro-sim; jeve's version would replace the fixed formula with its decision model. Also: when the proxy is the world's canonical resolver, its biases *are* the world's laws.

**X-COM [V, Gollop interviews].** Geoscape's "smallest time increment is 5 seconds"; aliens run region-level mission structures; Battlescape spawns on contact; Gollop: the two layers "didn't come together very well until very late in the project", yet "the whole experience is the sum of its parts" [V]. Write-back = soldier deaths/XP, recovered artefacts, score→funding [M]. **Lesson:** integration is the schedule risk; the write-back must be rich (typed multi-field), not a scalar.

**Battle Brothers [V]:** no autoresolve — "rather balance the game so that there are no meaningless battles." **Lesson:** an alternative governance: generate fewer encounters and always run them fully.

## 3. Social-simulation systems

**Façade — Mateas & Stern, "Structuring Content in the Façade Interactive Drama Architecture", AIIDE 2005, https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/18722 [V].** "Thousands of joint dialog behaviors" in ABL organised into "story beats sequenced by a drama manager"; beats give "rich, robust local interaction" while the manager "globally sequences beats chosen from a large pool to make story tension rise and fall" [V]. **Lesson:** beat = bounded multi-party episode with preconditions, internal reactive rounds, and exit; the drama manager is the budget/selection layer.

**Comme il Faut / Prom Week — McCoy, Treanor, Samuel, Reed, Mateas, Wardrip-Fruin, AIIDE 2010 (https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12454); FDG 2011 "Prom Week: social physics as gameplay" (DOI 10.1145/2159365.2159425) [V].** Four components: "Characters, the Social State, Social Exchanges and … Trigger Rules"; "A social exchange encapsulates a social action a character takes with another character (with the intention of changing their relationship) as well as how the other character responds"; >40 exchanges [V]; influence rules compute volition [V]; ~5,000 rules [M]. Ported to Skyrim as the Social NPCs mod (arXiv 2207.13398) [V]. **Lesson:** the closest *data model*: initiator intent → responder accept/reject (a typed boolean) → effects → trigger rules propagating to third parties. That trigger-rule ripple is the "fold back".

**Versu — Evans & Short, IEEE TCIAIG 2014, https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6648395/ [V].** Social practices are "reactive joint plans, providing affordances to the agents who participate"; they "never control the agents directly; they merely provide suggestions"; agents use "utility-based reactive action selection" [V]. **Lesson:** the practice is the *frame* (roles, legal moves, exit conditions) while the agent's decision function stays unchanged — exactly the separation jeve needs so the same decision model serves macro and micro.

**Talk of the Town / Bad News — Ryan, "Curating Simulated Storyworlds", UCSC 2018, https://escholarship.org/uc/item/1340j5h2 [V]; Samuel, Ryan, Summerville, Mateas, Wardrip-Fruin, ICIDS 2016, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-48279-8_10 [V].** Town simulated "day by day" 1839→1979 with "social exchanges and the internal lives of characters … beliefs, memories" [V]; Bad News layers a live human wizard/actor over one resident encounter [V]. Encounter outcomes are not folded back [M]. **Lesson:** the macro sim is a *situation generator*; the high-res encounter is where fidelity matters; the absent fold-back is the gap jeve would fill.

**Storylets — Kreminski & Wardrip-Fruin, ICIDS 2018, DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-04028-4_14 [V].** Narrative assembled from "discrete, reorderable narrative chunks"; four dimensions: precondition style, repeatability, content type, "content selection architecture" [V]. **Lesson:** a sub-sim invocation is a storylet; reuse the taxonomy for its selection policy.

**Off-screen worlds.** Dwarf Fortress world activation [V]: calendar "advanced by two weeks when you begin"; sites "conquered, liberated, defended, destroyed, reclaimed"; armies move "for site invasion, harassment, searching for work, and reclamation"; Adams' GameAIPro2 ch. 41 [V link]. The Sims 3 story progression: unplayed Sims "get promotions, get married, have children, make enemies, and even die" — replaced by NRaas because players judged it implausible [V/M]. CK3 replaced mean-time-to-happen with `on_action` because MTTH "made it difficult to govern how frequently events spawn, causing statistical anomalies and performance issues" [V]. RimWorld's storyteller picks incidents from the current situation with threat-point gating [V]. Nemesis: de Plater called the early version "overcomplicated", with too much "emphasis on Orcs' personal relationships", and cut it [V]. Radiant AI was scaled back "to keep the game understandable; otherwise a player might assume their items disappeared due to a bug" [V]. Kenshi has "World States" but Hunt refused a dynamic economy: "extremely hard to balance, and this world is already unstable enough" [V]. Elite's BGS: per-system 100 % influence, daily tick, action buckets, states [V]. **Lessons:** trigger on actions not timers; aggregate typed actions per tick; cut relational complexity that isn't legible; micro→macro feedback destabilises economies.

## 4. Distributed-simulation scoping

**Aura/nimbus — Benford & Fahlén 1993; Greenhalgh & Benford, MASSIVE, ACM TOCHI 2(3) 1995, DOI 10.1145/210079.210088 [V].** "Each object's aura defines the total region within which it interacts"; "communication is controlled by the observing object's focus and the observed object's nimbus" [V]. **Lesson:** aura collision is the group-formation trigger; focus/nimbus give *asymmetric, per-medium* membership (someone can be in earshot but not in conversation).

**Locales — Barrus, Waters, Anderson, IEEE CG&A 16(6):50–57, 1996 (MERL TR96-16a "Locales and Beacons") [V].** "Most of what a single user can observe at a given moment is nevertheless local in nature" [V]. **Lesson:** a locale is a bounded region with its own event stream — the cafe table as a locale.

**HLA DDM — Liu & Theodoropoulos, ACM CSUR 46(4) 2014, DOI 10.1145/2535417 [V]**: DDM "report[s] intersections between subscription and update regions". **Lesson:** which sub-sim events reach the macro world = subscription ∩ update region.

**Multi-resolution modelling — Davis & Hillestad, WSC 1993 pp. 1003–1012 [V]; Reynolds, Natrajan, Srinivasan, TOMACS 1997 [V].** Consistency is "the comparison between the projected state of an aggregate of high-resolution entities which evolved in M, and the projected state of the same aggregate initially controlled by M'" [V]. **Lesson:** this is the *test* jeve should run: aggregate outcome of the sub-sim vs. the proxy's outcome, over many seeds.

## (a) Closest precedent

No single system does "ad hoc small group → bounded high-res sub-sim → typed outcome folded into a persistent macro world". The nearest by component:

- **Architecture:** Bannerlord/Total War — a co-located group, self-contained round-based resolution, per-entity typed write-back, and a cheap proxy for the same event; but groups are armies, not conversations, and the proxy is a hand-tuned formula.
- **Social model:** CiF social exchanges (typed accept/reject, effects, trigger-rule ripple) inside Versu social practices (multi-party frame, agents keep their own decision function). Both have fixed casts, not co-location-formed groups.
- **Principle of cost/consistency:** Sunshine-Hill's perceptual simulation (sample high detail only when observed, conditioned on the macro prior, distributionally consistent) plus Reynolds & Natrajan's consistency test.
- **Trigger/membership:** aura/nimbus for who is in; locales for where the sub-sim lives.

## (b) Known failure modes

1. **Layer seams.** A-Life fights erupting at `switch_distance`; DF armies materialising abruptly; Total War autoresolve wiping whole units vs. spread casualties; MRM "chain disaggregation" where one expanded entity forces neighbours to expand [M]. 
2. **Proxy exploitation / selection bias.** Players choose manual vs. auto per battle to arbitrage biases (Total War, Bannerlord's defender bonus); jeve has no player, but the analogue is systematic: encounters that *get* the sub-sim will have a different outcome distribution from those that get the proxy, biasing the economy unless the proxy is calibrated (MRM test) and the selection rule is orthogonal to outcome.
3. **Determinism.** Games' sub-sims are notoriously non-reproducible (save-scumming exists because re-rolls differ) [M]. Jeve must derive every sub-sim RNG stream from the encounter's causal id, and the sub-sim's finer clock must never observe macro state that changes mid-episode.
4. **Cost blow-up and illegibility.** CK3's MTTH anomalies; Nemesis and Radiant AI both cut relational richness because outcomes read as bugs; Kenshi refused feedback loops for stability.
5. **Proxy rejection.** Sims 3 story progression shows a bad off-screen proxy gets noticed and replaced.

## (c) What appears absent

- **Recursion.** No system nests episodes (sub-sim spawning sub-sub-sim); LOD systems have levels, narrative systems have one episode layer.
- **Ad hoc social groups.** Social sub-games (CiF, Versu, Façade) use authored casts; LOD groups (Osborne & Dickinson) are static; aura/nimbus gives the trigger but no episode semantics. Co-location-formed, multi-round social episodes with typed exits are unoccupied territory.
- **Learned inner loop.** Every social sub-sim is a rule/content base; none uses a cheap non-generative decision model as the round-by-round chooser.
- **Published calibration.** No game exposes a procedure calibrating proxy vs. full sim; the MRM literature defines the test but game practice tunes by patch and modders fix the rest.
- **Deterministic replay across layers.** Not addressed anywhere found.
- **Fidelity evidence.** Sunshine-Hill's guarantee is by construction; O'Sullivan ran perceptual studies for geometry/motion LOD; I found *no* A/B evidence that higher-resolution social sub-simulation improves believability of a persistent world. That is a measurable claim jeve could be the first to test — using the Reynolds & Natrajan aggregate-consistency comparison as the metric.
